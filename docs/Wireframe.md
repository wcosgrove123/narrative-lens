Screen 1: The Story Library (Dashboard)
This is the home screen the user lands on when they open the app. Its purpose is to show all their projects at a glance and allow them to start a new one.

Wireframe (Layout & Structure)
Header: A simple bar across the top of the screen.

On the far left: The app's title, "Visual Narrative."

On the far right: A primary button labeled "+ New Story."

Body: The main area is a grid of story previews.

The grid contains multiple rectangular cards, each representing one photo story.

Each card is composed of a large rectangle (for the cover photo) stacked on top of a smaller rectangle (for the story's title).

Footer: A subtle text link at the very bottom center: "View My Public Portfolio."

Mockup (Visuals & Feel)
Theme: A modern, focused "dark mode." The background is a deep charcoal gray (#1A1A1A) to make the photography stand out.

Typography: Use a clean, sans-serif font like Inter. The app title in the header is bold and white. The story titles on each card are a slightly dimmer off-white (#E0E0E0).

Components:

The "+ New Story" button is the main call to action. It has a solid, vibrant blue background (#007AFF) with white text to draw the user's eye.

The story cards have slightly rounded corners. When a user hovers over a card, it should subtly glow or lift with a soft shadow to indicate it's interactive.

The cover images on the cards have a very soft, dark gradient overlaid on the bottom, ensuring the white story title is always readable.

Screen 2: The Story Editor
This is the core workspace where your brother will build his narratives. The design should be functional and distraction-free.

Wireframe (Layout & Structure)
Header: Contains navigation and the primary action.

Far left: A "< Back" icon/link to return to the Library.

Center: The editable title of the current story.

Far right: A toggle switch labeled "Publish."

Body (Two-Column Layout):

Left Column (The Canvas - 75% width): This is a vertical, scrollable list of the photos in the story. Each photo is a large rectangle with a multi-line text area for a caption directly below it.

Right Column (The Control Panel - 25% width):

Top Section ("Photo Bin"): A button to "+ Import Photos" sits above a small grid of unused, imported photo thumbnails.

Bottom Section ("Tone Editor"): This section is context-aware. It's empty by default but becomes populated when a user clicks a photo in the Canvas. It contains labeled horizontal lines for sliders (Brightness, etc.) and a row of small squares for filter presets.

Mockup (Visuals & Feel)
Theme: Continues the dark mode from the dashboard. The separation between the two columns is a very faint, thin line.

Typography: The story title in the header is large and bold. Caption text is smaller and uses a regular weight to feel secondary to the image.

Components:

When a photo in the Canvas is selected, it gets a thin, vibrant blue border to show it's the active target for the Tone Editor.

The sliders in the Tone Editor are styled with blue tracks and white handles.

The filter presets are circular thumbnails that show a miniature preview of the effect on the selected photo.

The "Publish" toggle is gray when off and flips to the vibrant blue when on, providing clear visual feedback.

Screen 3: The Public Portfolio Page
This is the clean, professional, public-facing website that displays the finished work.

Wireframe (Layout & Structure)
Header: A simple header containing the photographer's identity.

Left: The photographer's name in a large, prominent text block.

Right: Small text links or icons for "Contact," "Instagram," etc.

Body:

A short, centered paragraph of text directly below the header for the bio.

A grid of published story cards, visually identical in structure to the main app's library (large image rectangle over a title rectangle). Each card links to a viewer page for that story.

Mockup (Visuals & Feel)
Theme: A stark, minimalist "light mode." The background is pure white (#FFFFFF) or a very light gray (#F7F7F7) to emulate a clean art gallery wall. Text is a dark, readable gray (#333333).

Typography: The photographer's name should use an elegant, slightly more decorative serif font (like Lora or Playfair Display) to convey professionalism. The bio and story titles revert to the clean sans-serif font for readability.

Components:

The layout is defined by generous white space. Everything feels uncrowded.

The story cards have a very subtle, diffused drop shadow to make them feel like they are floating just above the page.

Links and hover-states use the same vibrant blue accent color from the app, creating a consistent brand identity between the creation tool and the public showcase
